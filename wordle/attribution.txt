# Vocabulary sources and attribution

The original English and German hardness CSVs were supplied for this study and are preserved without changes under `original/`. No new license is asserted for those source features.

The `wordfreq_*` fields in `words_en.csv` and `words_de.csv` derive from **wordfreq 3.1.1**, by **Robyn Speer**, using its large English and German data files. The transformation intersects recorded source tokens with the supplied vocabulary, maps German spellings forward to five A–Z cells, converts recorded frequencies to Zipf values, and preserves each matching measurement. `provenance.json` identifies source and output checksums. This does not recalculate any supplied score.

Wordfreq code is Apache-2.0; its frequency data is distributed under [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The derived frequency fields retain that license. See [wordfreq](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq), its [license](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq/blob/v3.1.1/LICENSE.txt), and its [source notices](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq/blob/v3.1.1/NOTICE.md).

Credit also belongs to the underlying sources named by wordfreq: Google Books Ngram Viewer ([data](https://books.google.com/ngrams)), Wikipedia, the University of Leeds Centre for Translation Studies' Internet Corpus, ParaCrawl, and the OpenSubtitles project through OPUS OpenSubtitles 2018. **Marc Brysbaert and the SUBTLEX coauthors** created the SUBTLEX frequency lists. SUBTLEX is freely available data from the [Ghent University Center for Reading Research](http://crr.ugent.be/programs-data/subtitle-frequencies). Full upstream source attributions and citations are retained in the linked wordfreq notices.

Suggested wordfreq citation: Robyn Speer (2022), *rspeer/wordfreq: v3.0 (v3.0.2)*, Zenodo, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.7199437](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7199437). The actual software/data package used here is pinned separately to version 3.1.1.
